      ## Music Groups in Lancashire - Right on Your Doorstep

      You might live in Tottington or Edenfield, work in Blackburn, have family in Preston, or be reading this in Darwen, Helmshore, Haslingden or Rawtenstall wondering whether anything like this actually happens nearby. It does - and it's closer than most of Lancashire realises. Our four weekly sessions run in Ramsbottom, Greenmount and Bury, communities that sit just over the county border and have been Lancashire in spirit for as long as anyone can remember. Mill towns, market days, brass band stands - the tradition of making music together never really left around here.

      Since 2021, Uke Group North has been running sessions where people who have never touched an instrument come along, borrow a ukulele, learn a couple of chords and end up strumming and singing with a room full of new friends. There are no auditions, no sheet music and no age limits - our members range from school-age children to people in their 80s. It's the friendliest thing you could do with a Tuesday evening or a Monday morning.

      The instrument? The ukulele. If that sounds a bit silly, stay with us - it's precisely why complete beginners end up playing real songs in their very first session, and it's why so many people who never thought of themselves as "musical" now wouldn't miss a week.

      Four sessions run every week, and wherever you are in East Lancashire or the Ribble Valley, one of them will suit your week:

      ## What Goes On at a Session

      Nobody in the room expects you to know anything on your first night - here's the shape of an ordinary session:

      1. **A warm welcome at the door.** We show you where to sit, hand you a ukulele if you've asked for one, and point you towards other first-timers.
      2. **Tuning, together.** Two minutes, all done for you if you like.
      3. **Two easy chords.** The ukulele has four strings, which keeps the start shallow. That's genuinely all it takes to play your first song.
      4. **The songbook comes out.** Familiar tunes from folk standards to recent pop, everybody strumming and singing as loudly or quietly as they please.
      5. **Home by the finish time,** having spent an evening actually making music instead of just hearing about someone else's.

      There's a bit of technique and theory sprinkled in for anyone curious, and experienced players get fuller arrangements to get their teeth into. Singing is collective rather than solo, and nobody is ever asked to perform alone.

      ## Why the Ukulele Wins People Over

      We understand the scepticism - plenty of members arrived feeling it. But the ukulele earns its place as our group instrument for practical reasons:

      - **Four strings mean simple chords,** and simple chords mean you're playing songs in week one rather than month six.
      - **Nylon strings are kind to fingers,** so there's no painful guitar-style breaking-in period.
      - **It's small and portable** - sling it in the car for the trip over from Helmshore or Preston.
      - **Even a beginner's strum sounds cheerful,** so there's no demoralising "horrible noise" phase.
      - **It's built for accompanying voices,** and singing together is where the magic of a group really lives.

      The pattern we see over and over: someone comes along "just to try it", gets hooked on the group first and the instrument second, and a few months later owns a ukulele of their own. If you get bitten by the bug, there's no ceiling - but there's no pressure either.

      ## What to Bring

      - **Nothing at all is fine.** Ukuleles are free to borrow - just mention it when you book so we set one aside.
      - **Your own ukulele if you have one,** any size or condition. We'll help you get it tuned.
      - **The songbook,** if you like things on paper - grab the [UGN Songbook (PDF)](/assets/UGN_Songbook_1.1.pdf) for a tablet or print it out. We keep paper copies at every session too.
      - **A sense of humour about your own singing.** Honestly the only requirement.

      ## Frequently Asked Questions

      **I live in Rawtenstall or Haslingden - which session is closest?**
      The two Ramsbottom groups are practically on your doorstep: Tuesday evenings at The Den is a short drive down Stubbins Vale Road, and Tuesday mornings at the library is barely further. Edenfield and Helmshore folk are equally well placed.

      **Is Ramsbottom really Lancashire?**
      Historically, absolutely - and sentimentally, its people would say the same. Our sessions sit right along the county border, in communities shaped by the Lancashire mill towns and market days around them. Wherever you travel from in the county, you'll get a proper Lancashire welcome.

      **Can I come over from Preston or Blackburn for a session?**
      Yes, and some members do. The Thursday evening group in Bury town centre tends to work best for longer journeys, as it runs late enough to arrive after work. Allow around 45 minutes to an hour from Preston or Blackburn by car, with free parking in Bury after 6pm.

      **I've never played anything in my life. Is that a problem?**
      Not remotely - it's the norm. Most of our members arrived without a single note of experience, and first-timers play a real song together in session one using two easy chords.

      **Will I be forced to sing on my own?**
      Never. Singing here is collective - a room full of voices, not a solo spot. Many people hum along at first and find themselves joining in properly within a few weeks, entirely at their own pace.

      **Is there an age limit, young or old?**
      No. Members run from children to people in their 80s, and that mix is one of the things that makes the group what it is.

      **What if I can only make it occasionally?**
      Come as often as life allows. There's no membership, no attendance record and no long-term commitment - you simply pay £8 for each session you attend, whenever that is.

      **Do the group perform in public?**
      Occasionally, at small local events - always voluntary, always low-key. Some members relish it, others never take part, and both are equally part of the group.
